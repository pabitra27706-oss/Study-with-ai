3.2 node_modules is committed in at least two repos

matriq/backend/node_modules contains framer-motion, motion-dom, html-to-image — and matriq is 29 MB as a result. club-web is 46 MB. CodeC is 31 MB. That is what makes the repos slow to clone, and it is the kind of thing a reviewer notices immediately.

Fix: add a real .gitignore, then git rm -r --cached backend/node_modules and commit. History stays fat, but the working tree shrinks instantly. If you care about the history, git filter-repo (Termux: pkg install git-filter-repo) rewrites it — do that once, on a clean clone.

4. The two assets you already own that are worth more than a new one

4.1 Jelet-prep — do not start a new JELET repo

It already exists and it is better than anything I would have proposed from scratch:

  • syllabus.md — the full JELET-2026 syllabus, matching the official Information Bulletin of 18 March 2026 (Maths 40 Q / 50 M, Physics 30 Q / 35 M, Chemistry 20 Q / 25 M, FEEE 10 Q / 10 M; Cat-1 +1/−0.25, Cat-2 +2 all-correct with partial credit).
  • pyq-md/ — 2017, 2018, 2019, 2021, 2022, 2024, 2025 transcribed in Markdown with LaTeX.
  • pyq-index.html — marked + MathJax renderer.

What is missing is not content, it is instrumentation. It has 2 commits and stopped on 4 June 2026 — the day the 2026 exam was about to be written. It was abandoned, not finished. The three things that turn it into a real prep system:

  1. A scoring engine that reproduces the official rule, including the tie-break chain (fewest negatives → Maths → Physics → Chemistry).
  2. An error log with a forced classification per wrong answer: concept / careless / misread / time. Without this, 500 practice questions produce no learning.
  3. A topic heat map ranking by marks recoverable, not by accuracy.
