26:Ta4c,<h2 id="01-the-question">01 — The question</h2>
<p>I had spent six evenings reading about HTTP caching with the help of an AI
tutor, taking notes, writing summaries. It felt productive. So the Sunday test
was simple: explain how <code>Cache-Control: max-age</code> and <code>ETag</code> interact, from
memory, in writing, with no AI and no notes open.</p>
<h2 id="02-my-attempt-before-asking-anything">02 — My attempt (before asking anything)</h2>
<p>I could describe <code>max-age</code> — it is how long a response stays fresh, in
seconds. I could describe an <code>ETag</code> — a version identifier for a resource. And
then the question I actually needed to answer: what happens when a browser has
a fresh copy but the server&#39;s resource changed?</p>
<p>I wrote: &quot;The browser sends the ETag, the server compares, and if it is
different the server sends the new content with a new ETag.&quot; And then I stopped,
because I did not know <em>what status code</em> came back, or whether the browser was
supposed to send the ETag at all in that request.</p>
<h2 id="03-where-i-was-wrong">03 — Where I was wrong</h2>
<p>Two things, and they are different in kind:</p>
<p><strong>First, a knowledge gap.</strong> I did not know the answer. Fine — that is what the
test is for.</p>
<p><strong>Second, and worse: a process failure.</strong> I had read the material <em>six times</em>
and had never once tried to recall it. Every session started by asking the AI
to explain something, which produces the <em>feeling</em> of a fresh, complete
understanding — but familiarity is not availability. Research on the testing
effect puts the retrieval-practice advantage around g ≈ 0.50–0.61, which is
large; and the transfer effect (being able to actually use it) is smaller at
d ≈ 0.40. I was collecting the first number and paying for it with the
second.</p>
<h2 id="04-what-changes">04 — What changes</h2>
<p>From now on, the loop is: <strong>read → close the tab → write what I remember →
then open the AI and check.</strong> The AI&#39;s job moves to the end of the session
instead of the beginning. If I cannot write three sentences before the AI
speaks, I have not studied the thing yet.</p>
<p>I also started a <code>cards/</code> folder for the handful of facts that kept slipping
(Cache-Control directives, status codes, header semantics) — those go into
spaced repetition. Facts that need understanding go into prose notes; facts
that need recall go into cards. Mixing them was my other mistake.</p>
<p>Next week: same test, same rules. The number that matters is the second one,
not the first.</p>
