The first closed-book test was worse than I expected

45 minutes, no AI, no notes — and I could not reproduce most of what I had 'learned' the week before.

The mistake

I mistook the feeling of understanding for the ability to produce it.

01 — The question

I had spent six evenings reading about HTTP caching with the help of an AI tutor, taking notes, writing summaries. It felt productive. So the Sunday test was simple: explain how Cache-Control: max-age and ETag interact, from memory, in writing, with no AI and no notes open.

02 — My attempt (before asking anything)

I could describe max-age — it is how long a response stays fresh, in seconds. I could describe an ETag — a version identifier for a resource. And then the question I actually needed to answer: what happens when a browser has a fresh copy but the server's resource changed?

I wrote: "The browser sends the ETag, the server compares, and if it is different the server sends the new content with a new ETag." And then I stopped, because I did not know what status code came back, or whether the browser was supposed to send the ETag at all in that request.

03 — Where I was wrong

Two things, and they are different in kind:

First, a knowledge gap. I did not know the answer. Fine — that is what the test is for.

Second, and worse: a process failure. I had read the material six times and had never once tried to recall it. Every session started by asking the AI to explain something, which produces the feeling of a fresh, complete understanding — but familiarity is not availability. Research on the testing effect puts the retrieval-practice advantage around g ≈ 0.50–0.61, which is large; and the transfer effect (being able to actually use it) is smaller at d ≈ 0.40. I was collecting the first number and paying for it with the second.

04 — What changes

From now on, the loop is: read → close the tab → write what I remember → then open the AI and check. The AI's job moves to the end of the session instead of the beginning. If I cannot write three sentences before the AI speaks, I have not studied the thing yet.

I also started a cards/ folder for the handful of facts that kept slipping (Cache-Control directives, status codes, header semantics) — those go into spaced repetition. Facts that need understanding go into prose notes; facts that need recall go into cards. Mixing them was my other mistake.

Next week: same test, same rules. The number that matters is the second one, not the first.
