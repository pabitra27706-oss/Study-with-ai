26:Tb16,<h2 id="01-the-question">01 — The question</h2>
<p>I kept hitting the same wall: a chat window full of good explanations, and
nothing I could point to a month later. Notes lived in one app, code in
another, half-formed thoughts in the chat history itself. The AI was doing the
remembering for me, and AI conversations do not age well.</p>
<h2 id="02-my-attempt">02 — My attempt</h2>
<p>I asked the AI to design a better system for me. It produced a tidy diagram:
ingest → summarise → store → review. Clean. And I noticed immediately that it
had removed the only part that was actually mine — the attempt. The pipeline
had no place for me to be wrong, and being wrong is the part that produces the
learning.</p>
<h2 id="03-where-i-was-wrong">03 — Where I was wrong</h2>
<p>Two corrections, and they came from the evidence rather than from taste.</p>
<p><strong>The guarded-tutor result.</strong> In a real course with GPT-4 assistance, students
who used the tutor freely improved their <em>practice</em> performance (about +48%)
but performed <strong>17% worse unaided</strong> on the exam. The harm disappeared when the
tutor was constrained — hints instead of answers, quizzes instead of lectures.
So the design constraint is not &quot;AI tutor&quot;, it is &quot;AI that cannot answer the
question for me&quot;.</p>
<p><strong>The effort trap.</strong> Open-ended chatbot tutoring pushes people into the
cheapest available action — accept the answer. A study of this found the
sequence Prime → Probe → Point → Attach → Strengthen → Test: the learner
generates the question, attempts it, gets the explanation, connects it,
consolidates it, and is finally tested. Each step is <em>more</em> work than the
last, which is exactly the point.</p>
<p>So the repository — not the chat — is the spine. A commit is a dated,
immutable record of what I understood at a specific moment. If I cannot
express it in a file, I did not understand it yet.</p>
<h2 id="04-what-changes">04 — What changes</h2>
<p>Concrete rules now in <code>AGENTS.md</code>:</p>
<ul>
<li>The AI reads <code>raw/</code> and <code>notes/</code>; it <strong>never</strong> writes <code>journal/</code>. That
folder is mine.</li>
<li>Every session ends with a commit. Not a summary — a decision.</li>
<li>The weekly test is closed-book and no-AI. It is the only score that counts.</li>
<li><code>raw/</code> is append-only. Notes are AI-compiled and I own them. Cards are
curated by me — roughly a third of what the AI generates survives.</li>
</ul>
<p>One more thing I am keeping: the AI is allowed to disagree with me and to
flag contradictions between my notes. A tutor that only agrees is a mirror,
and mirrors do not teach.</p>
<p>Next: write the actual <code>AGENTS.md</code> and make the loop runnable rather than
aspirational.</p>
