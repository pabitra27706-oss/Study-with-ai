Why the repository is the spine and the AI is only the tutor

I had been treating the repo as an archive of notes. It is not an archive — it is the only place where my understanding is stored in a form I can audit.

The mistake

I was using the AI to produce content instead of using it to test content I had produced.

01 — The question

I kept hitting the same wall: a chat window full of good explanations, and nothing I could point to a month later. Notes lived in one app, code in another, half-formed thoughts in the chat history itself. The AI was doing the remembering for me, and AI conversations do not age well.

02 — My attempt

I asked the AI to design a better system for me. It produced a tidy diagram: ingest → summarise → store → review. Clean. And I noticed immediately that it had removed the only part that was actually mine — the attempt. The pipeline had no place for me to be wrong, and being wrong is the part that produces the learning.

03 — Where I was wrong

Two corrections, and they came from the evidence rather than from taste.

The guarded-tutor result. In a real course with GPT-4 assistance, students who used the tutor freely improved their practice performance (about +48%) but performed 17% worse unaided on the exam. The harm disappeared when the tutor was constrained — hints instead of answers, quizzes instead of lectures. So the design constraint is not "AI tutor", it is "AI that cannot answer the question for me".

The effort trap. Open-ended chatbot tutoring pushes people into the cheapest available action — accept the answer. A study of this found the sequence Prime → Probe → Point → Attach → Strengthen → Test: the learner generates the question, attempts it, gets the explanation, connects it, consolidates it, and is finally tested. Each step is more work than the last, which is exactly the point.

So the repository — not the chat — is the spine. A commit is a dated, immutable record of what I understood at a specific moment. If I cannot express it in a file, I did not understand it yet.

04 — What changes

Concrete rules now in AGENTS.md:

  • The AI reads raw/ and notes/; it never writes journal/. That folder is mine.
  • Every session ends with a commit. Not a summary — a decision.
  • The weekly test is closed-book and no-AI. It is the only score that counts.
  • raw/ is append-only. Notes are AI-compiled and I own them. Cards are curated by me — roughly a third of what the AI generates survives.

One more thing I am keeping: the AI is allowed to disagree with me and to flag contradictions between my notes. A tutor that only agrees is a mirror, and mirrors do not teach.
